01 Two maps

One railway, drawn two ways.

Every station in the dataset has two positions: where it really is, and where it sits on the hand-drawn control-room diagram. RailFlow keeps both and moves between them.

The change doesn't cross-fade. It starts at Delhi and travels down the line, the way a state change moves through a real railway. Same geometry, same draw call; one number differs.

02 The price of a section

Routing on minutes, not kilometres.

Each bar is one section's expected running time right now: its length over its line speed, raised as trains crowd onto it. Dijkstra searches over these prices, recomputed against live occupancy. That's what turns map routing into traffic control.

minutes = km ÷ line speed × 60 × m

m = 1                    load ≤ 0.7
m = 1 + (load − 0.7)×1.4  load ≤ 1
m = 2 × load             load > 1

03 A closure

Close Moradabad–Rampur. Watch the search.

Each ring is a station Dijkstra settled, drawn in the exact order the algorithm settled it, with the minutes it assigned. The green line is the route it returned.

Any train whose path crossed the closure gets rerouted, but only if the new route is projected to be faster. If the controller can't show an improvement, it does nothing.

 

04 Down to the rail

A train is a length, not a dot.

Following a train, live. Each vehicle is placed by arc length along the alignment, so a formation bends through a curve coach by coach instead of sliding along as one block.

Trains lean into curves by v²/r, capped at the six degrees real track allows. The console runs the same motion against the backend's stream, at frame rate between one-second snapshots.

 

05 Your turn

Close any section.

Click a section to close it; click it again to reopen it. The controller reroutes whoever needs it, once per simulated minute, highest priority first. Drag to move around, shift-drag to rotate.

    How it's built

    One loop, one lock, one minute at a time.

    1. Tick

      An asyncio task advances the world by one simulated minute. Everything lives in memory, behind a single lock.

    2. Move

      The integrator spends time, not distance. A train that reaches a station mid-tick carries the leftover time onto the next section, at that section's speed.

    3. Occupancy

      Counted once per tick. It sets each train's speed and each section's price.

    4. Decide

      At most one controller decision per tick: reroute, hold or stop. It must be valid, then projected to help.

    5. Broadcast

      A snapshot goes out over WebSocket. The console dead-reckons between snapshots, so motion is continuous at frame rate.

    On the reinforcement learning

    What exists, and what doesn't.

    RailFlow ships a Gymnasium environment over the live simulation, a PPO training entry point on Stable-Baselines3, and the safety gate above, which would contain a bad policy.

    It does not ship a trained policy that makes decisions. The PPO path hands off to the rule-based controller, and every number on this page comes from that controller. The documentation explains why the current formulation wouldn't learn, and what it would take to fix it.
